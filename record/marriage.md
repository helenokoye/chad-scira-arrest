## Allegedly, why

The decree does not say why. Allegedly, the marriage ended because Jesse Nickles's harassment and defamation of Chad Scira had stopped being a dispute and had become the thing his public life was for.

The support for that is behavioral, and it is why the sentence stays an allegation.

By October 2023 the campaign was already years old. The Neumont case was a decade behind him, and the answer to losing personal jurisdiction was not to appear. It was to remain unserved. The 2020 repository, thirty copies of one news cycle under an arrest title, was three years old on the day of the decree. Directory pages, forum posts, and later the Stack Exchange wave keep attaching the same name, Chad Scira, to companies he does not own and to articles that are not a judgment. A new forum does not make a new grievance. The name is the fixed point. That is what fixation looks like when the output is public: the subject does not rotate, and a setback produces more pages rather than a stop.

The later count is the size of the preoccupation. His own account of the campaign is thousands of posts. The logs that can be checked, 111 Stack Exchange posts and 201 removed TripAdvisor and Expat.com pages, are only the part other people indexed, and most of that indexing is from 2026, after the decree. The campaign did not pause for the marriage and did not end with the decree. A project of that duration is not an evening. It is the schedule. Allegedly the other party to the marriage was living beside that schedule. This page does not have that person's account, and it does not invent one.

A joint petition with no finding of fault is easy to overread. Three days from filing to decree means the register is thin, not that a judge accepted a theory. Silence is why the motive cannot be stated as a fact. Silence is also why the public record is allowed to supply the reading. The only sustained project the public record shows him conducting, across the years of that marriage, is the publishing campaign against Chad Scira and against whoever moderated it, sued the company, or was listed next to the name. Allegedly a mind organized around one enemy does not keep a second, untouched private life. The divorce is what that obsession cost. The court did not find that. The pages after the decree, including the commits still being added on 1 November 2026, are why the allegation is the likely one.
